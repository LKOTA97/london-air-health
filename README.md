# London air pollution and health

What is a sustained 1 µg/m³ reduction in annual mean PM2.5 worth in each London borough, in quality-adjusted life years (QALYs)? And how much could be spent each year to achieve it while staying cost-effective?

This project answers that with open data from OHID Fingertips and the Office for National Statistics, a DuckDB database queried with SQL, a life-table model in R, and a Quarto dashboard.

**Live dashboard:** <https://lkota97.github.io/london-air-health/>

The answer is framed as a break-even budget, not as the value of one scheme, because the cost and exposure effect of any single scheme are the weakest inputs in most air quality evaluations. Any proposed scheme (Healthy Streets, school streets, wood-burning controls, low-emission zones) can be compared with the break-even figure.

## Key findings

1. **London's air has improved but is still above the WHO guideline.**
   - Population-weighted PM2.5 fell from 12.3 µg/m³ in 2018 to 8.9 µg/m³ in 2024, against 7.1 µg/m³ for England. The WHO guideline is 5 µg/m³.
   - In 2024, 6.6% of deaths at ages 30 and over in London were attributable to PM2.5 (5.3% in England).
   - More deprived boroughs tended to have higher PM2.5 (correlation 0.65 across 32 boroughs, IMD 2025). That is an ecological pattern, tangled with inner-London geography.

2. **A sustained 1 µg/m³ cut is worth about 82,670 discounted QALYs across London.**
   - That is 318,407 life-years undiscounted, or about 21 days for each resident aged 30 and over.
   - At £20,000 per QALY, NICE's figure for public health guidance, London could spend up to **£116.3m a year for 20 years**, or £12.75 per resident a year, and stay cost-effective.
   - The 95% interval from hazard-ratio uncertainty is £10.47 to £15.04 per resident.
   - Across boroughs the figure runs from £10.52 per resident (Tower Hamlets) to £14.39 (Richmond upon Thames). Boroughs differ by age structure, not by how polluted they are, because the relationship is linear with no threshold.

3. **The annual figure depends most on how the spending is framed and discounted.**
   - If the spending buys a cut that then lasts, London's break-even is £198.8m a year when spread over 10 years but £89.9m a year over 30 years, a difference of more than a factor of two.
   - If the cut lasts only while it is paid for, the break-even barely moves: £64.1m to £70.5m a year.
   - The Green Book's discounting rules, revised in the Budget of 28 October 2026, raise the figure to £254.1m a year, 2.18 times the base case. They discount at 3% a year and uprate health values by 2% a year, in place of the old, lower health discount rate. The base case keeps NICE's 3.5%.
   - The hazard ratio's 95% interval spans £88.1m to £130.3m.

## How the model works

- **Intervention:** a hypothetical, permanent cut of 1 µg/m³ in annual mean PM2.5 across each borough.
- **Effect:** all-cause death rates fall by the COMEAP (2022) hazard ratio of 1.08 per 10 µg/m³, linear with no cut-off. The benefit is phased in with the US EPA cessation lag: 30% in year 1, 80% by year 5, 100% by year 20.
- **Who:** residents aged 30 and over in mid-2025, by sex and single year of age, followed to age 100 with England's 2022-2024 life table. Extra life-years are weighted by EQ-5D-5L population norms (NICE's preferred value set since August 2026) and discounted at 3.5% (NICE).
- **Break-even:** discounted QALYs times a cost-effectiveness threshold give the most that could be spent in today's money. Spreading that over H years gives the annual figure: `annual = PV / sum(1 / 1.035^t, t = 1..H)`.
- **Uncertainty:**
  - one-way sensitivity analysis: hazard ratio, lag, discounting, cohort, life-years against QALYs, borough mortality, horizon;
  - a probabilistic analysis with 5,000 draws of the hazard ratio.
- **Checks:**
  - The model's life expectancy at 30 is within 0.03 years of ONS for both sexes.
  - A test confirms the official Fingertips attributable fractions use the same 1.08 coefficient.
  - An independent re-implementation in Python matched the R model in all nine scenarios.

Every parameter, with the page or paragraph of its source, is in [`model/parameters.csv`](model/parameters.csv). The reasons for each choice are in [`notes/decision_log.md`](notes/decision_log.md).

## Reproduce

You need R 4.3 or later (built with R 4.6.1) and Quarto 1.4 or later (built with 1.10.19). On Windows:

```powershell
winget install --id RProject.R -e
winget install --id Posit.Quarto -e
winget install --id Git.Git -e
```

Then, from the project folder:

```r
renv::restore()        # installs the locked package versions as ready-built binaries
```

```powershell
Rscript R/run_all.R    # downloads data, builds the database, runs the model, sensitivity analysis and tests
quarto render          # builds the dashboard into docs/
```

`R/run_all.R` stops if any test fails. The raw downloads are committed in `data/raw/`, except two EQ-5D spreadsheets that have no open licence; the scripts download those.

## Limitations

- **Deaths only.** Illness, children's health and NHS savings are not counted, so the results are likely conservative overall. Extra years are valued at average population quality of life, which may be too high for the people whose deaths are delayed.
- **Whose opportunity cost.** NICE thresholds reflect the NHS's opportunity cost. A council spending its own public health grant faces a different trade-off.
- **Small cuts only.** The value per µg/m³ is roughly constant for small cuts. Do not scale it up to large cuts without re-running the model.
- **Same death rates everywhere.** England death rates are used for every borough unless the SMR scenario is on. Residents are followed as if they never move.
- **Narrower population than COMEAP.** The base case counts residents aged 30 and over. COMEAP's own method follows the whole current population.
